How The Edge picks — the model

The ranking uses price and nothing else — but since 30 July 2026 two solvency screens decide which companies are eligible to be ranked (step 3 below). The core signal is 12-1 momentum — the last 12 months of return, skipping the most recent month:

ret12−1 = ( Pt−1mo / Pt−12mo ) − 1

The most recent month is skipped deliberately: over that horizon stocks tend to reverse, so including it works against the signal. This replaced the old acceleration signal in July 2026, after acceleration was falsified on survivorship-free data.

Each rebalance (monthly, on the first trading day):

  1. Universe — top ~1,000 US names by market cap (Russell-1000 proxy).
  2. Liquidity floor — keep only names ≥ $10B market cap. Momentum degrades as the floor drops — at $200M this same model loses badly to the index — so the floor is load-bearing, not a convenience.
  3. Solvency screens — keep only companies generating positive free cash flow and carrying less than 4× EBITDA in debt. Added 30 July 2026; it cut the worst simulated drawdown from −46% to −29% for about a third of a point of annual return. Screens based on valuation were tested and made the model worse — this is about whether a business can survive a bad year, not whether its stock looks cheap.
  4. Rank by 12-1 momentum, highest first — the ranking itself uses price and nothing else.
  5. Sector cap — at most 2 of the 10 from any one sector. Without it the book once held 9 technology names, which is really one bet wearing ten badges.
  6. Hold the top N equal-weight (selector above; product = 10 stocks, the 7-stock research book is paper-tracked alongside).
  7. Regime switch — if the S&P 500 is below its 200-day average, cut to 25% invested (rest in cash). Checked every day, not only on rebalance day.
  8. Volatility target — scale the whole book down whenever its own recent swings run hotter than a 25%-a-year pace. This covers what the regime switch structurally cannot: the two worst drawdowns in this model's history happened while the S&P was above its 200-day average and falling only ~10%. The market was fine; the momentum stocks were not.
  9. Costs — returns are shown net of ~10bps trading costs, including the cost of changing exposure.

Honest caveats: short-window returns are lumpy and tail-driven, and the recent era has been unusually momentum-friendly — trust the risk-adjusted shape (Sharpe, drawdown) over the headline CAGR. History is now survivorship-free (delisted companies included), which cut the measured edge substantially versus earlier versions of this page.

Note: signals are formed at one close and entered at the next one, so the model never trades at the price it used to decide. The product runs a single sleeve — staggering was tested and dropped (it cost 0.8pts).

Forward record — the only out-of-sample evidence

A rebalance counts here only if it was written down before its outcome was known. The “logged” column is the wall-clock time this system first recorded that basket — that timestamp, not the book date, is what makes a row genuine evidence, so rows that fail the test are marked and excluded rather than quietly counted. The record restarts whenever the model's parameters change, so it is deliberately short.

The “Nd after” note beside a timestamp is the gap between the date a book is dated and the moment it was actually written down. For real forward evidence that gap is zero. A row marked ⏳ backfilled has a large one — it was recorded after its holding window had already started, so scoring it would report a return over a period that had already happened. Those rows are shown but never scored, and never counted in any total.

Every row currently in this table is backfilled, stranded, or superseded, which means the forward record has not scored a single rebalance yet. That is the honest state of it: the solvency screens shipped on 30 July 2026 and reset the record. What is accruing evidence is the live mark above, running from 30 July with the current book — the first scoreable rebalance closes at the next month-start.

A row marked ↺ superseded was replaced by a later book for the same date after a change to the selection rules — the 2026-07-01 book was first written down under the pre-solvency-screen model, then re-picked once those screens shipped. The original stays visible because it is a genuine record of what was written down at the time; it is simply no longer what the model holds.

A row marked ⊘ stranded was written down under a rebalance clock this model no longer runs, so no closing date exists for it and it can never be scored. Moving to monthly month-start rebalancing on 30 July 2026 stranded the books dated mid-month. They stay visible rather than being deleted — but they are shown as stranded, not as still open, because a row that can never close is not a live position.
