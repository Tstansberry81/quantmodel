1 Universe & eligibility

Each rebalance date \(d\): keep the top-1,000 US names by point-in-time market cap (a Russell-1000 proxy), then apply a tradeable-size floor. \(\delta=90\) days is the fundamentals lag.

$$\operatorname{rank}\big[\operatorname{mcap}_i(d-\delta)\big]\le 1000 \quad\text{and}\quad \operatorname{mcap}_i(d-\delta)\ge \$2\text{B}$$

2 Signals (market data only)

Price momentum over several horizons; acceleration is the only one the live model scores on — "is momentum speeding up?" \(P_i(t)\) is the total-return adjusted close.

$$\text{ret}^{(k)}_i=\frac{P_i(t)}{P_i(t-k)}-1,\qquad k\in\{21,63,126\}$$
$$\text{accel}_i=\underbrace{\left[\frac{P_i(t)}{P_i(t-63)}-1\right]}_{\text{last 3 months}}-\underbrace{\left[\frac{P_i(t-63)}{P_i(t-126)}-1\right]}_{\text{prior 3 months}}$$

Also computed but unweighted in the product: \(\text{hi\_252}=P_i(t)/\max_{[t-251,t]}P_i\), relative strength \(\text{rs\_63}=\text{ret}^{(63)}_i-\text{SPX}^{(63)}\), 21-day volatility, and market beta.

3 Cross-sectional score

Standardize each signal across all eligible names that day (z-score), then take a weighted blend. The product weights only acceleration.

$$z_{c,i}=\frac{x_{c,i}-\mu_c}{\sigma_c},\qquad s_i=\sum_c w_c\,z_{c,i}\;\xrightarrow{\;w=\{\text{accel}:1\}\;}\;\boxed{\,s_i=z(\text{accel}_i)\,}$$

4 Selection — growth blend + correlation cap

Split the \(n=10\) slots by the growth mix \(m\): \(K\) names must come from the revenue-growth pool, the rest from anywhere. Both are filled greedily by score, subject to the correlation cap.

$$K=\operatorname{round}(m\cdot n)=\operatorname{round}(0.75\times 10)=8,\qquad G=\{\,i:\ g_i(d-\delta)\ge 0.15\,\}$$

Walk candidates best-score first; admit \(i\) into the chosen set \(C\) only if it isn't too correlated with anything already held (Pearson over the trailing 126 daily returns):

$$\text{admit } i \iff \max_{j\in C}\big|\rho_{126}(r_i,r_j)\big|\le 0.50$$

Take 8 from \(G\) and 2 from the full eligible set this way (backfill by score if the cap leaves too few). This revenue-growth filter \(g_i\) is the model's only fundamental input.

5 Execution, weighting & regime overlay

Execution lag. The signal is formed on the close of day \(t\), but the trade enters on the next close \(t{+}1\) — you can't compute the whole cross-section and also trade the close it was computed from. Positions are then held \(h=42\) trading days (a ~2-month clock), buy-and-hold (weights drift with returns, they are not re-set daily).

$$\text{signal from }P_i(t)\;\Rightarrow\;\text{enter }P_i(t{+}1),\quad\text{hold }h=42\text{ days},\quad w_i(t{+}1)=\tfrac{1}{n}$$

The 200-day regime switch cuts exposure to \(\theta=0.25\) (75% to cash, earning the risk-free \(r_f\)) on any day the S&P is below its 200-day average. The state is evaluated daily across the holding window (at the prior close, so it's causal) — not frozen at the rebalance — so a fast intra-window crash de-risks immediately rather than riding it out:

$$r_d=\begin{cases}r^{\text{book}}_d & \text{SPX}(d)>\text{MA}_{200}(d)\\[6pt]\theta\,r^{\text{book}}_d+(1-\theta)\,r_f & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

Staggered sleeves. The book is run as two sleeves offset by half a period (\(h/2\approx 21\) days), combined 50/50, so performance doesn't depend on the luck of a single rebalance calendar:

$$r^{\text{prod}}_d=\tfrac12\,r^{(\text{sleeve A})}_d+\tfrac12\,r^{(\text{sleeve B, }+h/2)}_d$$

6 Transaction costs → net return

Turnover is the fraction of the basket replaced versus last rebalance; cost \(c=10\) bps is charged on it.

$$\tau_d=1-\frac{|C_d\cap C_{d-1}|}{|C_d|},\qquad r^{\text{net}}_d=r_d-\frac{c}{10^4}\,\tau_d$$

7 Equity curve & reported metrics

Performance is measured on the daily net-return series \(r^{\text{net}}_d\) of the combined staggered book — so the drawdown reflects true intra-period peak-to-trough, not just values sampled every rebalance. Annualize with 252 trading days over \(T\) days.

$$\text{eq}_T=\prod_{d=1}^{T}\!\left(1+r^{\text{net}}_d\right),\qquad \text{CAGR}=\text{eq}_T^{\,252/T}-1$$
$$\text{Sharpe}=\sqrt{252}\;\frac{\overline{r^{\text{net}}}}{\sigma(r^{\text{net}})},\qquad \text{maxDD}=\min_t\!\left(\frac{\text{eq}_t}{\max_{s\le t}\text{eq}_s}-1\right)$$

Overfitting-adjusted checks. Across the 120 configs actually screened, and measured on non-overlapping 42-day periods (the daily series holds each book 42 days, so overlap autocorrelation makes a daily-basis test invalid): the product's excess vs the S&P is significant under Newey-West HAC errors, \(t=+2.17\) (raw \(t=+1.71\)); the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (deflating for the trial count + fat tails) is \(\approx1.00\); and the Probability of Backtest Overfitting sits right on the 0.5 danger line and is not stable — the same test on the same config measured \(0.67\) on one data vintage and \(0.49\) a week later. We therefore treat PBO as inconclusive rather than passing: the honest reading is that the \(n\)/growth-mix dials are not robustly optimizable (near-identical configs, so the selection test cannot identify a persistent winner), which is a caution against dial-mining rather than proof the acceleration signal itself is overfit. Note the shipped config ranks #59 of 120 on composite score — it is a fixed economic choice, not the in-sample winner. For transparency, the same tests on the statistically invalid daily basis read weaker still (\(t=+1.56\), PBO \(0.63\)).

In one sentence

Each rebalance, take the top-1,000 large-caps above $2B, rank them by acceleration (z-scored), fill 8 slots from ≥15%-revenue-growth names and 2 from anywhere — each admitted only if <0.50 correlated with names already in the book — enter the next day and hold equal-weight for ~2 months, run as two half-period-staggered sleeves, cut to 25% invested on any day (checked daily) the S&P is below its 200-day average, and net out 10 bps of turnover cost.

Honest caveat: the universe is missing ~89% of delisted names (survivorship), the liquidity floor uses market cap as a full-history proxy, and the recent window has been momentum-friendly. On survivorship we now have a direct test rather than an assumption: rebuilt on Norgate's real point-in-time Russell 1000 — every constituent as of each date, including the 50 names that later delisted, priced straight through their delisting — the model returned 28.5%/yr against the S&P's 16.3% over 2024–26, i.e. removing the bias did not shrink the edge (it was wider than the proxy universe's 22.7%). That test is only ~2 years / 9 rebalances and runs pure-momentum, since that dataset carries no fundamentals, so it is a directional green light, not proof for the full history. Drawdowns are now measured daily (true intra-period, ~−26% vs the S&P −55%, with the 200dMA de-risk evaluated daily), and staggering removes the calendar luck a single sleeve carried. Trust the risk-adjusted shape (Sharpe ≈0.9, drawdown control) over the headline return; ~70% of it is buyable factor premium, not proprietary alpha.
